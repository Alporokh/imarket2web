Local SEO for a one-person studio: what actually works

You are one person with a chair, a calendar and maybe two hours a week for marketing. Most local SEO advice is written for businesses with a marketing department. This guide is for you, in the order I would do the work.

By Alena Porokh, SEO specialist, imarket2web · Last updated: September 2026 · 9 min read

Why does the map pack matter most for a local studio?

For a studio serving one city, the map pack is worth more than everything else in this guide combined. Those three results sit above the organic links and carry a star rating and a distance.

On a phone, they fill most of the first screen.

So the honest order is: fix what decides map pack position first, then work on your website. That is the opposite of how most agencies sell it, because a website is a bigger invoice than a profile.

If you have two hours a week, spend the first six weeks on your Google Business Profile and reviews. Nothing else you can do in that time comes close.

Step 1: How do you set up a Google Business Profile properly?

Complete every field once, accurately, and keep it current. Google uses completeness to judge whether it knows enough about you to recommend you with confidence.

Most studio profiles are about 60% complete and have been for two years. Completeness is not a vanity metric here.

  • Name: your real business name. Not "Studio Name - Permanent Makeup Poznań Best Prices". Keyword stuffing breaks Google's guidelines, and competitors can report it.
  • Hours, including exceptions. A profile that says open when you are shut produces one-star reviews from people who drove across town.
  • Services, listed individually with prices. Each one is something people search for.
  • Attributes: accessibility, parking, languages spoken, appointment required. These feed filters people really use.
  • Booking link, pointing wherever you actually take bookings.

This is one afternoon of work, done once. It is the highest-return afternoon in local marketing.

Step 2: Which Google Business Profile category should you choose?

Choose as your primary category the service you most want to be found for, not the broadest description of your business. The primary category shapes what you rank for more than any other field.

Get it wrong and the rest of the work fights uphill.

A studio that does permanent makeup and nails picks one of them as primary, based on which pays better. It should not pick "Beauty Salon", which puts it in a pool with every salon in the city.

Secondary categories cover the rest. Add the ones that genuinely apply and stop there, because irrelevant categories dilute rather than add.

How to check which categories your competitors use

It is public if you know where to look. Open a competitor's profile, view the page source, and search for the category strings.

It is tedious, but it shows exactly which category the businesses ranking above you use. That is better evidence than any guess.

Step 3: When should you ask customers for reviews?

Ask at the moment the customer is happiest with the result, which is often not when they walk out the door. The timing matters more than the wording.

Every guide tells you to ask for reviews. Almost none tell you when, and the when decides whether you get one.

  • Permanent makeup: after the healing week, when the color has settled and they have had the first compliment.
  • Massage: that same evening.
  • Tattoo: once it has healed and they have shown it to someone.

Ask through the channel you already use with them. If bookings run through Booksy or WhatsApp, ask there, not by an email address they gave you once.

  • Reply to every review, including the good ones. Everyone deciding whether to book can see your replies.
  • Reply to bad reviews calmly, in public, once. Then move the conversation to a private channel. The reply is for the next person reading, not for the reviewer.
  • Never buy reviews. A sudden burst of five-star reviews from accounts with no history is the pattern Google is best at detecting. The penalty lands on the profile you depend on.

A steady trickle beats a burst. Ten reviews over ten months is a stronger signal than ten in a week, and it looks more honest to people reading them.

Step 4: Why does each service need its own page?

A single price list can rank for only one thing and answers nobody's specific question well. A separate page per treatment can match its own search and answer its own doubts.

This is where the website earns its place. The most common mistake is one price list with every treatment on it.

Someone searching for lip blush and someone searching for removal of another artist's bad work are different customers. They have different fears, budgets and urgency. One page cannot serve both.

Give each treatment its own page, and write it the way people actually ask:

  1. What it is, in their words rather than the trade term
  2. Whether it hurts, and what you do about that
  3. How long it lasts and what the healing week looks like
  4. What it costs (a range is fine, silence is not)
  5. What happens if they do not like the result

The last three are the questions that arrive in your DMs. Answering them on the page ranks for long-tail searches and removes the doubt that was stopping the booking.

Step 5: Which photos actually help a local business?

Recent, specific photos help most: your entrance, your room, healed results and you. Profiles with recent photos get more engagement, and engagement feeds back into visibility.

The useful version is narrower than "post more photos":

  • The exterior, from the direction people approach. This is the photo that stops someone walking past your door.
  • The room. For a first treatment, the feeling of the space is a big part of the decision.
  • Healed results, not fresh ones. Fresh work photographs better and disappoints later.
  • You. A single-practitioner studio's advantage is that it is you every time. A chain cannot promise that.

What local SEO tasks waste your time?

Mass directory listings, daily rank checks, posting for its own sake, endless homepage rewrites and bought backlinks rarely pay off for a small local business.

  • Citation building at volume. Paying to be listed in 200 directories was useful a decade ago. Now a handful of real local directories is enough.
  • Checking your rank daily. Local results change with the searcher's exact location. What you see in your own studio is not what a customer sees two districts away.
  • Posting for the sake of it. A Google Business post that supports nothing you sell costs twenty minutes and returns nothing.
  • Rewriting your homepage again. If the map pack is where customers decide, the homepage is not the bottleneck.
  • Buying backlinks. For a local studio these rarely move local rankings and sometimes cause real damage. Earning a few relevant links is a separate, slower piece of work.

What order should you do local SEO in with two hours a week?

Profile and categories first, then reviews, then service pages, then photos and replies on a monthly rhythm. Analytics and a content calendar come last.

  1. Weeks 1-2: complete the profile and fix the categories.
  2. Weeks 3-6: build a review habit, asked at the right moment.
  3. Weeks 7-12: one page per treatment, best-selling first.
  4. Ongoing: new photos monthly, and a reply to every review.
  5. Only then: analytics, a content calendar and everything else.

None of this is quick. All of it compounds. The profile you complete this month keeps working while you build pages, and reviews keep arriving while you do nothing at all.

How long does local SEO take?

Category changes can move things within weeks. Reviews and pages take months. If someone guarantees you the map pack by a date, ask what happens when they miss it.

Frequently asked questions

Is local SEO worth it for a one-person business?

Yes, if your customers come from one city or district. The map pack shows three businesses above the regular results, and a complete profile with steady reviews is how a small studio gets into it.

How many hours a week does local SEO take?

Two hours a week is enough if you follow the order above. The profile is one afternoon of work. Reviews and replies take minutes each week, and service pages are written one at a time.

Do I need a website for local SEO?

You can start without one, because the map pack depends mostly on your Google Business Profile and reviews. A website becomes important in weeks 7-12, when each service needs its own page.

Should I buy reviews or backlinks?

No. Bought reviews follow a pattern Google detects well, and the penalty hits the profile you depend on. Bought backlinks rarely help a local studio and can cause damage.

How soon will I see results?

Category fixes can show within weeks. Reviews and service pages usually take several months to build a steady position in the map pack.
