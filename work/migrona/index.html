Migrona OÜ: a B2B freight forwarding SEO case study from Tallinn

Migrona OÜ has no map pack, no reviews to collect and no impulse bookings. Its buyer compares four forwarders on reliability and insurance before emailing any of them. I used the same eight layers as for the studios, assembled in a completely different order.

What was the problem?

In B2B freight, the website is not the salesperson. It is the reference check a buyer runs before starting a conversation, and a vague services page disqualifies a forwarder on the spot.

There is no walk-in trade and no Google Business Profile worth optimizing. Nobody leaves a five-star review for a pallet that arrived on time. They simply book again.

The buyer is a logistics manager or a business owner with cargo to move. They compare three or four forwarders on reliability, document handling and insurance. The decision is made before anyone picks up a phone.

What I built

I built a multilingual B2B website that states each service precisely, puts capability and reliability first, and gives buyers a direct route to a person.

  • Website and service architecture. Each freight service findable and specific enough to be quoted against.
  • B2B positioning. Capability and reliability up front, because the buyer is qualifying a supplier, not browsing.
  • Multilingual build. English, Estonian and Russian, matched to how the corridor actually operates.
  • Trust and credentials. Insurance, customs competence and the hub network presented as reasons to shortlist.
  • Enquiry route. A direct path to a person, because a freight enquiry is a conversation about a specific consignment.

Why does B2B freight need a different order of work?

Local search carries a beauty studio, but it is nearly worthless for a freight forwarder. Credibility, service specificity and language move to the front instead.

Services stated precisely enough to be quoted

A freight buyer does not search for "logistics". They search for the exact shape of their problem:

  • International road freight. The core service, made credible by the routing and hub network.
  • Groupage. Part-load consignments, a different buyer from someone booking a full truck, with different price sensitivity.
  • Oversized cargo. The specialism that separates capable forwarders from brokers, and the search with the least competition.
  • Vehicle transport. Its own market, with its own vocabulary and worries about damage in transit.
  • Customs clearance and documentation. On Baltic and EU-border routes, often the reason a forwarder is chosen at all.
  • Cargo insurance and warehousing. The questions a serious buyer asks third, and often the ones that decide it.

The hub network is the proof

Rotterdam, Magdeburg, Vilnius and Kyiv answer the buyer's first real question: can you move my cargo on my corridor, or will you subcontract it? So the network is presented as operational capability, not as an address list.

Three languages, because freight runs in three

English is the contract language. Estonian is the home market and the regulatory language. Russian is the working language across much of the corridor this company serves.

For a forwarder, language coverage signals who you can actually do business with.

Trust, adapted for B2B

The studio projects answer "will this hurt, and will it look right?". This one answers "what if my cargo is damaged, who handles the border paperwork, and are you insured?". The principle is the same: the objection goes on the page, before the contact form.

What are the results so far?

No figures are published yet. B2B freight has long sales cycles and low enquiry volume, so the honest metrics are enquiry quality and ranked service-level queries, not traffic.

They will be published once there is enough history to mean something.

What comes next?

Corridor and route content is the largest remaining opportunity. These are pages built around specific lanes, such as Estonia to the Netherlands or the Baltics to Ukraine, which is how freight demand is searched.

After that comes service-level SEO for oversized cargo and customs clearance, where competition is thinnest and buyers are most committed.

Why is a freight forwarder in this portfolio?

It is the control experiment. If the method worked only for beauty studios in Poznań and Prague, it would be a niche, not a method.

Freight forwarding shares none of their surface features and still responds to the same discipline. Find out how demand is phrased, build the structure to match it, answer the objection before the form, and only then think about design.
