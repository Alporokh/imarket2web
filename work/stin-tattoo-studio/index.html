Stin Tattoo Studio: a tattoo studio SEO case study from Prague

Nobody books a tattoo from a paragraph. They book it from an image that makes them think: that is the line, that is the hand. For this artist-led studio in Malá Strana, I made the portfolio the sales page and organized it by style.

In short: the gallery works as navigation, one landing point per tattoo style. Aftercare content answers healing worries and ranks for them. The artist, not the studio, leads the positioning.

What was the problem?

The studio sits in one of the most-walked districts in Europe, but that foot traffic is mostly tourists. The clients worth building for research a style for months and search by style, not by street.

A tourist booking a permanent tattoo on a city break is the least valuable, least repeatable client a studio can get. Serious clients save references and want a specific artist. They search for fine-line, ornamental or micro-realism.

Most tattoo sites are a gallery with a phone number. The gallery is right. The missing structure around it is what costs bookings.

What I built

I built a website with a portfolio structured by style and an artist profile that gives the reason to book. Aftercare content and a booking form for custom work complete it, in English and Czech.

  • Website with a style-led portfolio. The gallery works as navigation, not as an endless scroll.
  • Artist profile. The person and the working method, positioned as the reason to book rather than as a biography.
  • SEO and local search. Style-based search terms alongside Prague and Malá Strana signals.
  • Aftercare section. Practical healing guidance that also covers a large set of searches.
  • Consultation-first booking. A form built for custom work, where a conversation must happen before a price can exist.
  • Bilingual build. English and Czech, for two audiences that search in different words.

Why organize a tattoo portfolio by style?

Style is the unit people search in and the unit they decide in. When each style is a landing point, a visitor arriving from a style search sees that work first instead of scrolling a mixed feed.

The portfolio is split into six styles:

  • Fine-line and minimal. The largest search volume, the most crowded competition, and the style most often booked as a first tattoo.
  • Ornamental and floral. Highly visual and shareable; this work most often arrives through saved references.
  • Graphic and illustrative. Here the artist's own drawing voice does the selling, not the technique.
  • Traditional and neo-traditional. A knowledgeable audience that judges a studio on whether the linework holds up.
  • Blackwork and lettering. Searched with different vocabulary, and with different expectations around cover-ups and scale.
  • Micro-realism. Color and black-and-grey are kept apart, because they are two different skills and get compared as such.

Why aftercare content earns its place

Aftercare is usually a PDF handed over at the door. On the site it does three jobs. It calms the healing anxiety that stops people booking, ranks for high-volume searches and brings existing clients back.

Bringing past clients back to the site is the cheapest reputation work a studio can do.

Why lead with the artist, not the studio

In an artist-led studio, the artist is the brand. Anna's profile carries the practice: the approach to custom design, the consultation and the atmosphere.

"Cozy" is not decoration in this market. For a first tattoo, the feeling of the room is a large part of the decision.

What are the results so far?

No figures are published yet, because Search Console needs more history first. Results will appear here with the date range they cover.

What will be measured: ranked style queries, entry pages by style, and consultation form completions split by language.

What comes next?

Analytics comes next, so the map pack and the style pages can be judged on consultations booked, not on impressions. After that, new work gets published by style on a steady rhythm.

That rhythm is the closest thing a tattoo studio has to a content engine that maintains itself.
