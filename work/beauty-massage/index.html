Where it started

A single-practitioner studio cannot win on price, opening hours or advertising budget. It has exactly two advantages over a chain, and both of them are usually buried at the bottom of the homepage: the depth of the technique list, and the fact that the same pair of hands does every treatment.

The third factor in Prague 4 is language. Nusle has a large Czech population, a large Russian-speaking population, and an international one on top. Those are three distinct markets with three distinct ways of searching for the same massage.

A chain sells a treatment. A specialist sells the reason to book that treatment with her specifically. Only one of those survives a price comparison.

The approach

Two structural decisions did most of the work.

One — face and body are different businesses

The face work here is unusually deep: facial ritual, lymphatic drainage, myofascial and lifting massage, gua sha, buccal massage, alginate mask, head massage. That is a treatment ladder people book repeatedly and search for by name, often as an alternative to aesthetic clinic procedures.

The body work — sports, classic, anti-cellulite, honey, bamboo whisk, cupping, reflexology, back, aromatherapy, lymphatic drainage — is bought for different reasons on a different rhythm, frequently as a one-off for a specific complaint.

Merged into one list, both get weaker. Separated, each becomes findable by the people already searching for it.

Two — the practitioner is the proof

Ten years of practice and certification is the single strongest trust signal the studio has, and it is the one thing a chain fundamentally cannot reproduce — because a chain cannot promise you which employee you get. So the person is not tucked into an "about" page as a formality; she is presented as the reason the technique list is credible in the first place.

The language decision
Three languages, not one site translated twice

Czech, English and Russian speakers in Prague 4 use different words for the same treatment and weigh different things when choosing. Buccal massage, gua sha and lymphatic drainage each carry different levels of familiarity across the three. Each version is written for its own audience rather than run through translation on top of the Czech.

What got built

  • Website and SEO architecture — face and body treatments split into their own findable structures.
  • Trilingual build — Czech, English and Russian, each written for how that audience searches.
  • Practitioner positioning — certification and a decade of practice used as the spine of the site, not as a footnote.
  • Local content — Prague 4 and Nusle signals, so the studio competes in its own district before it competes with the city.
  • Price list and gift vouchers — structured as their own entry points, since both are searched directly.
