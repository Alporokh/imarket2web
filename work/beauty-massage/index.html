Beauty Massage: a single-practitioner SEO case study from Prague

Beauty Massage is one certified masseuse with ten years of practice, competing with wellness chains that outspend her on every channel. I built her trilingual site around the two things a chain cannot copy: her technique list and the fact that she does every treatment herself.

In short: face and body treatments are split into separate structures. The practitioner's certification and ten years of practice are the spine of the site. Each language version is written for its own audience.

What was the problem?

A single-practitioner studio cannot win on price, opening hours or ad budget. Its two real advantages were buried at the bottom of the homepage, and three language markets were served by one approach.

The first advantage is the depth of the technique list. The second is that the same pair of hands does every treatment.

Language is the third factor in Prague 4. Nusle has a large Czech population, a large Russian-speaking population and an international one. They are three markets with three ways of searching for the same massage.

A chain sells a treatment. A specialist sells the reason to book that treatment with her. Only one of those survives a price comparison.

What I built

I built a trilingual website with separate structures for face and body treatments. The practitioner is the central trust signal, and Prague 4 signals come first. Prices and gift vouchers are entry points of their own.

  • Website and SEO architecture. Face and body treatments split into their own findable structures.
  • Trilingual build. Czech, English and Russian, each written for how that audience searches.
  • Practitioner positioning. Certification and a decade of practice used as the spine of the site, not as a footnote.
  • Local content. Prague 4 and Nusle signals, so the studio wins its own district before it competes with the city.
  • Price list and gift vouchers. Built as separate entry points, because both are searched for directly.

Why split face and body treatments?

Face and body work are bought for different reasons on a different rhythm. Merged into one list, both get weaker. Separated, each becomes findable by the people already searching for it.

Face work is a repeat-booking ladder

The face work here is unusually deep: facial ritual, lymphatic drainage, myofascial and lifting massage, gua sha, buccal massage, alginate mask and head massage.

People book these repeatedly and search for them by name. They often compare them with aesthetic clinic procedures.

Body work answers a specific complaint

Sports, classic, anti-cellulite, honey, bamboo, cupping, reflexology, back, aromatherapy and lymphatic drainage massage are often one-off bookings for a specific problem.

Why make the practitioner the proof?

Ten years of practice and certification are the strongest trust signal the studio has. A chain cannot reproduce it, because a chain cannot promise which employee you will get.

So the practitioner is not tucked into an "about" page as a formality. She is presented as the reason the technique list is credible.

Why three languages, not one site translated twice

Czech, English and Russian speakers use different words for the same treatment and weigh different things when choosing. Buccal massage, gua sha and lymphatic drainage have different levels of familiarity in each group.

Each version is written for its own audience rather than translated from the Czech.

The review loop is already running. The Google Business Profile is the piece being finished. For a single practitioner, reviews and the profile together turn the trust advantage into something search engines can read.

Analytics comes after that, split by language, because the three markets almost certainly behave differently.
